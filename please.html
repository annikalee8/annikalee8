Beverage Co.

An integrated digital strategy designed to turn a strong local product and community into stronger discoverability, conversion and repeat purchase.

Academic team project. The strategy was developed for a live Vancouver beverage brand, but the recommendations, forecasts and financial scenarios shown here are project work — not claimed implementation results.

The brief

A good product does not help much if the digital path to purchase is weak.

The initial audit found a brand with real-world community momentum — including events and a tasting room — but a thin digital acquisition and measurement foundation. Search visibility was concentrated in generic terms, paid search was inactive, and the site had no established SEO, analytics or A/B testing system.

Strategy architecture

Build one connected system, not a list of disconnected tactics.

The recommendation sequence was designed so each channel improves the next one: strengthen the site, optimize conversion, capture active demand, create new demand, retain customers and recover visitors who leave.

SEO + CRO

Make high-intent discovery useful by improving what happens after the click.

The SEO plan shifted focus toward non-branded purchase-intent searches such as canned cocktails in Vancouver, ready-to-drink cocktails in Canada and low-sugar cocktails. On-page recommendations included stronger titles, product descriptions, image alt text and technical improvements.

Traffic alone was not the goal. The team paired acquisition with A/B tests around landing-page structure, lifestyle imagery, product-page CTAs, pricing, email sign-up and social proof so the site could learn what actually improved user behaviour.

Paid search

Separate people who know the brand from people who are still deciding what to drink.

The proposed Google Ads structure used three campaign types: branded searches, non-branded product searches and event-based searches for the tasting room. That split allowed different intent levels to receive different copy, budgets and conversion goals.

Social + creators

Use the brand's personality to create demand before someone searches for it.

The social strategy leaned into emotion, humour and storytelling rather than treating follower count as the main objective. Proposed content included street interviews, first-sip reactions, event moments, founder and behind-the-scenes stories, and influencer content that could feel native rather than overly scripted.

The team also screened 20 Vancouver-based creators using engagement rate and proposed affiliate codes so conversion could be attributed by partner instead of judging creator activity only by reach.

Lifecycle

Turn a tasting-room visit or first purchase into an ongoing relationship.

The email plan covered six lifecycle moments: welcome, cart abandonment, event promotion, post-purchase, win-back and nurture. The retargeting model then handled visitors outside the email list, using reminder and benefit messaging first before introducing discount urgency later in the window.

Economics

Make the acquisition plan answer a basic question: can the customer be bought profitably?

Instead of recommending media spend without a guardrail, the team built a break-even model around average order value, margin, conversion rate and repeat purchase assumptions. The exercise showed why branded search could support a very different allowable CPA from broader non-branded acquisition, and why lifetime value matters before scaling.

All figures in this section are academic assumptions and projections. They are not reported financial results from Please! Beverage Co.

Takeaway
The strongest channel plan is the one where each part makes the next part work better.

This project was useful because it forced the team to connect strategy across the full funnel. SEO and paid search brought people in, CRO improved the destination, social created demand, email developed the relationship, retargeting recovered drop-off, and break-even logic put an economic constraint around the entire system.
