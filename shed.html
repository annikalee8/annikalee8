Consumer research / New product development / Opportunity analysis

A data-led concept for a reusable, modular pet-hair removal system designed around the moments existing tools leave unresolved.

Team university project. The concept remained academic; the research photographs shown here come from our original observational work.

The problem

Pet owners had learned to manage the problem, not solve it.

Our research started with the everyday reality of pet hair on clothes, furniture and hard-to-reach surfaces. Existing solutions such as lint rollers, brushes and vacuum attachments often worked only temporarily, required multiple tools or created waste. The project looked for a more complete opportunity rather than simply designing a slightly better lint roller.

Discovery process

Go beyond asking pet owners what product they wanted.

The project combined observation, inquiry and exploratory interviews. We looked at homes, dry cleaners, vacuum and clothing stores, public spaces and other contexts where pet hair appears indirectly. Early interviews sometimes confirmed our assumptions rather than uncovering new information, so the process shifted toward more open-ended prompts and deeper follow-up questions about why the problem mattered.

What changed the direction

The highest-value opportunities were more specific than “clean pet hair better.”

Opportunity scoring surfaced moments that were easy to overlook during initial brainstorming. Emergency removal before leaving the house ranked first. Hair returning after laundry and buildup in corners or vents also scored near the top, while the lack of an all-in-one solution reinforced the need for a modular system.

The concept

Contain the hair instead of redistributing it.

The final concept was a compact, reusable system with one handle and multiple cleaning functions. Its core design principle was containment: capture hair and hold it rather than moving it from one surface to another or sending it back into the air.

01

Emergency removal mode

A quick drag-and-capture surface for clothing before work, events or travel.

02

Pre-wash capture

Remove hair before laundry so it is less likely to redistribute across clean garments.

03

Corner & crevice tool

An angled attachment for baseboards, vents and other tight spaces that scored unexpectedly high.

04

Multi-surface heads

Different contact surfaces for garments, delicate fabrics and heavier upholstery without forcing one compromise.

05

Enclosed collection chamber

Hold captured hair until the user empties it instead of dispersing it during cleaning.

06

Washable & refill-free

A reusable design intended to avoid adhesive sheets and recurring lint-roller refills.

Trade-offs

Not every interesting problem deserved a feature.

The project deliberately excluded or deprioritized needs that scored lower, already had adequate workarounds or would push the product beyond a realistic consumer scope. Car upholstery, electronics and HVAC-spread hair were among the lower-priority areas. Survey results also redirected attention away from some allergy-focused ideas toward the more immediate daily needs customers were actively trying to solve.

The concept and opportunity scores are part of an academic new-product-development project, not a commercial product launch or tested market result.
Takeaway
Research changed the product more than brainstorming did.

One of the most useful lessons was seeing how quickly a team can become attached to technically interesting problems. The data repeatedly pulled the concept back toward ordinary, recurring frustrations: looking presentable before leaving, getting hair out before laundry, reaching neglected corners and avoiding a drawer full of single-purpose tools.
