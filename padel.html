PADEL IT!
VANCOUVER.

Market research / New venture / Strategy

A researched concept for a central, indoor padel club designed around the barriers Vancouver players said were keeping them off the court.

Team project. The page shows selected visuals from our original presentation deck.

The opportunity

A fast-growing sport with an access problem in the urban core.

Our concept began with a simple gap: Vancouver had strong awareness of padel, but limited convenient access to dedicated indoor courts. The project tested whether that gap was large enough to support a central, pay-per-play facility and what the customer experience would need to look like.

What we learned

The strongest barriers were practical, not a lack of interest.

01

Distance mattered

Interviewees repeatedly treated roughly 30 minutes as a hard travel limit, making location a core part of the value proposition.

02

Seasonality created a gap

Outdoor racquet activity drops through Vancouver's wetter months, making year-round indoor access a meaningful benefit.

03

Padel felt exclusive

Equipment cost and private-club perceptions created friction for first-time and casual players.

04

Community mattered

Research pointed toward bright, welcoming facilities and social spaces rather than a purely transactional court-rental experience.

The concept

Design the business around the barriers the research uncovered.

The proposed club combined four indoor courts with a pay-per-play model, a central Vancouver location, equipment rentals, coaching and a social component. The aim was to make padel feel accessible to people who were interested in the sport but unwilling to commit to a distant or exclusive private-club experience.

Go-to-market

Acquisition, booking and community were designed as one system.

The project mapped marketing channels alongside the way customers would actually discover, book and return to the facility. Digital booking was the primary distribution path, supported by on-site access and group or corporate sales to help fill off-peak capacity.

01

Digital discovery

Social, search and locally relevant promotion were positioned to build awareness among active students and early-career professionals.

02

Low-friction booking

An online court-booking platform was proposed as the primary purchase path for the target demographic.

03

Trial-friendly access

Equipment rentals and beginner-friendly entry reduced the need for an upfront commitment to the sport.

04

Community retention

Events, coaching, social spaces and later leagues were designed to turn first-time visits into repeat participation.

Financial thinking

Stress-test the idea beyond the marketing story.

We built operating assumptions, funding requirements, revenue streams, risk scenarios and a multi-year growth model. The financials were academic forecasts rather than operating results; their role was to test whether the concept could support the proposed customer experience and facility model.

Takeaway
The most useful part of the project was turning customer research into concrete choices about location, pricing, access and experience.

Padel It! brought together several parts of business that are often taught separately: primary research, segmentation, positioning, operations, marketing, financial modeling and risk planning. The finished concept was stronger because the customer insights shaped the business model rather than being added after it.
